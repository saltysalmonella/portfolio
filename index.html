Hexapod Robot

2025 — present

A six-legged robot learning to navigate on its own

A hexapod robot that extends an open source design from MakeYourPet. It drives 18 servos across six legs and runs ROS 2 Jazzy on a Jetson Orin Nano, with C++ firmware on a Pimoroni Servo2040 (RP2040) handling the low-level servo control over a USB binary protocol.

The control stack is deliberately modular — teleoperation feeds a gait generator, which feeds an inverse-kinematics solver, which feeds the servo driver. Each stage is its own ROS 2 node, so any piece can be tested in isolation. I re-derived the per-leg inverse kinematics for my own link geometry and implemented an alternating tripod gait for stable walking.

Current phase — autonomy

The robot has completed autonomous 40 cm square routes using onboard sensing and Nav2. In a September 2026 tethered trial, it completed all six route legs with a 0.10 m/s speed limit and finished about 8 cm from its starting centre, measured by onboard odometry. The next goal is continuous exploration and mapping of reachable rooms; whole-floor coverage is still in progress.

Inside the hexapod

From sensing to autonomous motion

I'm building the autonomy stack around the same gait and leg kinematics used for manual walking. Perception, planning, and motion control run onboard the Jetson, with a separate dashboard for monitoring trials.

  1. 01

    Locate the robot and build a map

    A RealSense D435i supplies stereo images, depth, and inertial data for cuVSLAM motion estimation. Filtered lidar feeds SLAM Toolbox for a 2D map, while nvblox reconstructs nearby geometry from depth. Together, these give the navigation stack a position estimate and maps of the space around the robot.

    Perception & mapping
  2. 02

    Plan around obstacles — and its own legs

    Nav2 plans paths and produces velocity commands that pass through a supervisor before reaching the gait controller. A hexapod's moving legs appear in its own sensors, so I use leg geometry to filter lidar and depth returns, and check clearance against the space the body and legs sweep through. A lift-scan-lower routine gives the lidar a clearer view before a route leg.

    Planning & geometry
  3. 03

    Keep the control loop responsive

    Running mapping and navigation alongside 18-servo control exposed scheduling delays on the Orin's six CPU cores. I moved the core control chain to C++, tuned mapping and planner workloads, and addressed blocking ROS communication. The supervisor pauses motion when navigation data becomes stale; persistent faults trigger a hold, with separate heartbeat, driver, and firmware watchdogs.

    C++ & reliability
  4. 04

    Turn short routes into room exploration

    Recorded physical trials now include completed square routes at several speed limits. The continuous mission layer selects frontiers — boundaries between mapped and unexplored space — and supports map checkpoints and route retries. I'm working toward reliable travel across multiple rooms and reuse of saved maps; those longer missions still need physical validation.

    Ongoing development
